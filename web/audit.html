Every claim, tried rather than read.

Alert's contracts had been tested against mocks and its page against no chain. The seam between them had never run once. This is that seam exercised: the shipped UI driving a real deployment through two complete trades, then six attacks on the result.

What this is not. Not a fork of Base Sepolia: the public RPC answers in about ten seconds from here and forking needs hundreds of calls, so USDC is a mock with the same six decimals rather than Circle's bytecode, and the Groth16 verifier is a mock that returns true. This therefore exercises the escrow, the UI, the hashing across two languages and the whole trade flow. It exercises no cryptography and no real token semantics. Both gaps are stated rather than implied.

Does the chain agree with the screen?

The seller typed ₦160,000 and the page allocated a kobo tag, displaying ₦160,000.33. The contract stores only a hash. If the page's hash and the contract's hash ever disagree, the user is told their correct amount is wrong, and nothing errors or logs.

That last row is the design claim that settle is callable by anyone because the proof is the authority, not msg.sender. A buyer with no gas can hand the proof to a relayer; the relayer cannot redirect the funds.

One test did not test what it claimed

The first replay attempt reverted with 0xddafad98, and I was about to record it as the nullifier guard. It is NotOpen. The escrow had already settled, so a different guard fired first and the nullifier was never reached. Proving it needed a second escrow, a different amount, and the same nullifier, which then returned 0xb115d857. A revert is not evidence that the guard you had in mind is the one that stopped it.

Findings

Medium · fixed

open() accepted a bank the deployment can never prove

open took bankDomainHash as an unvalidated parameter. A seller could lock USDC against kuda.com while the adapter is pinned to moniepoint.com, producing an escrow that is unsettleable by construction: funds stuck until the deadline with nothing on chain saying why. Found by mutation testing, not by review: removing the settle-time bank check left all 19 tests green, and chasing down why exposed the real hole one layer up.

Medium · fixed

refund was unreachable from the interface

The contract had it, the client module exported it, tests covered it, and no screen ever called it. A seller whose buyer never paid had no way to reclaim except by hand-crafting a transaction. Found by auditing the screen rather than the code.

Low · fixed

The direction guard moved without anyone noticing

While the amount was read from the plain-text copy of the email it anchored on the literal Debit Amount, so a credit alert could never match. Shrinking the circuit forced the regex onto the HTML copy, and Moniepoint styles both alert types identically, so a credit alert now matches the amount perfectly. What still excludes it is the recipient regex. The guard is now pinned by a test named for what it does, so dropping the recipient binding as "redundant" fails loudly.

Low · open

The body cutoff is a CSS fragment

To fit the circuit, the amount is extracted from the HTML copy anchored on 00; font-size: 20px; line-height: 57px;, where 00; is the tail of font-weight: 600;. It is the latest unique string before the amount and no semantic alternative exists. If Moniepoint restyles their email, every proof stops working. No fix is planned for the hackathon build; a production version needs a template-change monitor.

Info · open

The trust root is SendGrid, not Moniepoint

s1._domainkey.moniepoint.com is a CNAME into s1.domainkey.u8065049.wl050.sendgrid.net. Moniepoint's own selector is hosted by their mail provider, so there is no Moniepoint-controlled signing path for this mail stream. A trade counterparty cannot compromise SendGrid, which is the threat model that matters, but the accurate claim is "signed by Moniepoint's mail provider".

Info · open

Proof of sending, not of arrival

A proven debit alert establishes that the buyer's bank says money left their account. Nigerian transfers over NIBSS are near instant, but a transfer can debit and later reverse. This replaces a forgeable screenshot with a bank-signed attestation, which is a large improvement and is not a claim of final settlement.
