Reading the escrow contract…

Proofs are not verified in this deployment. The circuit is still compiling, so the escrow is wired to a stub verifier. The escrow, the amount binding, the replay guard and the refund path are all real. The cryptography is not.
Today
A screenshot, and hope

The buyer screenshots their bank app. The seller squints at it and decides whether to believe them. Forging one takes under a minute, which is why "fake alert" is slang here.

With Alert
The bank's own signature

Every bank email is cryptographically signed so Gmail can filter spam. That signature is checkable by anyone, forever, and the signer neither knows nor consents. Every bank is already an oracle.

No integration. No licence. No permission.

A smart contract holds the crypto leg perfectly and is completely blind to the naira leg. That blindness is the entire risk in this market, and every fix so far has been custodial: somebody holds your money and you hope.

Alert closes it without asking anyone. The bank cannot charge you, refuse you or cut you off, because the header it would have to withdraw is the one it needs in order to deliver mail at all.

  1. Seller locks USDC
    Into an escrow with no admin key.
  2. Buyer checks the figure
    Against the on-chain hash, before paying a naira.
  3. Buyer transfers naira
    Exact to the kobo. The amount is the binding.
  4. Proof releases the USDC
    Anyone can submit it. Only the buyer gets paid.

A bank alert says far more than the amount.

A real Moniepoint alert prints your account number unmasked, next to your balance. Settling a ₦160,000 trade should publish neither.

What the proof reveals

  • The amount, exactly as printed
  • That Moniepoint signed it
  • A one-time reference, so it cannot be reused
  • The last five digits of the destination

What stays private

  • The account holder's name
  • The full account number
  • The account balance
  • The counterparty's name
  • Every other line of the email

Non-custodial

No owner, no admin, no pause, no upgrade path, verified against the deployed bytecode rather than the source. USDC leaves on a valid proof or at the deadline. Nobody else can move it.

Proven, not asserted

Six attacks run against the live contracts. 50 tests, and a mutation suite that breaks each guard to confirm a named test catches it. Read the audit.

Honest about limits

It proves money was sent, not that it arrived. One bank today, and the buyer must bank somewhere that emails debit alerts. See the detail.
