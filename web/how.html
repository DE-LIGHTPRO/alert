One real trade

What actually happens, with the numbers it produced.

Not a description of the design. One trade, driven through the shipped interface, with the value each step returned.

Where this ran. A local chain, with the contracts that ship and the page that ships. USDC is a mock with the same six decimals, and the proof verifier is a stub returning true while the real circuit compiles. So the escrow, the interface, the hashing and the whole flow are real. No cryptography was exercised.

  1. The seller locks USDC

    They type the naira figure they want. The page does not use it directly: it finds a kobo value no open trade has claimed, because the amount is what ties an alert to this trade. Moniepoint overwrites the transfer narration with its own template, so a per-trade reference cannot ride along in it.

    seller typed ₦160,000 page quoted ₦160,000.33 kobo allocated on chain locked 100.00 USDC to account *****52988

  2. The approval is exactly the trade, and nothing more

    Checked after the lock. An unlimited approval would outlive the trade and is what a later compromise of the contract would inherit.

    allowance after locking 0 consumed exactly, nothing left over

  3. The buyer checks the figure before paying a naira

    The seller quotes a number out of band. Nothing stops them quoting one figure and opening the escrow with another, leaving the buyer unable to settle after the money is gone. The amount's hash is on chain, so the buyer can check the quote against it first. This is the cheapest fraud in the flow to prevent.

    ₦160,000.33 ✓ matches escrow #0 ₦160,000.34 ✗ does not match. Do not send anything. ₦160,000.00 ✗ does not match. 160000 neither. Type it as the alert will print it.
  4. The part that has to be exactly right

    The page and the chain agree on the amount

    The contract stores only a hash. The page computes its own. If those two ever disagree, a user is told their correct amount is wrong, nothing errors and nothing logs. So it is checked rather than assumed, in a live transaction.

    hash of the figure the page displayed 0x003111ca4ac75eace964a4cf93e2e732730c3f86ec2f8d3acbb2749e2dd253ea amountHash stored on chain 0x003111ca4ac75eace964a4cf93e2e732730c3f86ec2f8d3acbb2749e2dd253ea MATCH

  5. The buyer uploads the bank alert

    Read in the browser. The page shows what the proof will assert before anything is generated, and refuses a credit alert, which looks almost identical and proves the wrong direction.

    amount ₦5,000.00 recipient 52988 nullifier c4a88985eb621459bfd6e0ed92c07b50cd9740c8a043febb910f2688e979 the MIME boundary, 60 hex, inside a signed header credit alert instead ✗ rejected: no destination account
  6. The design claim, tested

    A stranger submits the proof, and the buyer gets paid

    settle is callable by anyone, because the proof is the authority and not the sender. A buyer holding no gas can hand it to a relayer. The relayer cannot redirect anything: the destination was fixed when the escrow opened.

    submitted by 0x90F7…b906 a third party, not the buyer buyer +100 USDC relayer 0 escrow 0 emptied

  7. And it cannot happen twice

    Replayed against a second escrow, with a different amount so the amount binding could not be the thing stopping it.

    same nullifier, escrow #1 reverted NullifierAlreadySpent fresh nullifier, escrow #1 settled, buyer paid again
